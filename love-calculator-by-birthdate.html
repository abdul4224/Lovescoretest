How this birth date calculator works

This tool uses the traditional numerology idea of a life path number. Every digit of a birth date is added together and the total is reduced down to a single digit from 1 to 9. The totals 11, 22, and 33 are kept as they are and are traditionally called master numbers.

Once both life path numbers are known, they're looked up in a fixed pairing table drawn from conventional numerology, which gives the base score. A small adjustment of a few points is then applied using the exact day and month you entered, so two couples who happen to share the same life path numbers don't all get an identical result. The same two full birth dates will always produce the same score.

Worked example

For 15 April 1990: 1 + 5 + 0 + 4 + 1 + 9 + 9 + 0 = 29, then 2 + 9 = 11. Because 11 is a master number, it stays as 11 rather than reducing again to 2.

An honest note on accuracy

There is no scientific evidence that a birth date predicts how well two people will get along. Numerology is a traditional belief system, not a tested method, and this calculator is built purely for entertainment. If you want something with actual grounding, the things that genuinely predict relationship satisfaction are ordinary and unglamorous: how you communicate, how you handle conflict, and whether you want similar things from life.

So enjoy the number, share it if it makes you laugh, and don't let it decide anything real.

In numerology, a life path number is found by adding together every digit of a birth date and reducing the total to a single digit from 1 to 9. The totals 11, 22, and 33 are traditionally kept as they are and called master numbers.

How is the compatibility percentage calculated?

Both birth dates are reduced to life path numbers, and those two numbers are looked up in a fixed traditional numerology pairing table. The result is adjusted slightly using the exact dates so different birthdays with the same life path numbers don't all return an identical score.
